# Digital circular plastics ventures in Sub-Saharan Africa

A mapped dataset of 26 organisations using digital technology in circular
plastics across ten African countries, with the sectoral systems of
innovation analysis built on it.

The dataset was compiled for a conference paper on the circular plastics
economy in Sub-Saharan Africa. It is published here separately because a
venture landscape is more useful as data than as an appendix table.

## The dataset

`data/ventures.csv` holds 26 organisations with, for each: name, country,
year founded, website, the digital technology recorded, and a summary of what
the organisation does.

| | |
|---|---|
| Organisations | 26 |
| Countries | 10 |
| Years founded | 1989 to 2019 |
| With a website recorded | 23 |

Nigeria accounts for 11 of the 26. Kenya has three; Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana,
South Africa and Zambia two each; Namibia, Tanzania, Rwanda and Uganda one
each.

The technologies recorded across the set are mobile applications, web
platforms and SMS, the Internet of Things, 3D printing and scanning, image
recognition, and points-based digital reward systems. Mobile applications
appear most often, typically as the interface between households, informal
collectors and recycling infrastructure.

### Data quality

Stated plainly, because it affects what the dataset can be used for:

- The `digital_technology` column is populated for only 3 of 26 rows in the
  source table. For the rest, the technology is described in the summary
  text but was not coded into its own field.
- One organisation has no founding year recorded.
- Two entries carry apparent typographical errors from the source and have
  been left as published rather than silently corrected.

The dataset reflects the landscape as surveyed for the 2023 paper. Ventures
in this sector open and close quickly, so the URLs and operating status
should be checked before the data is relied on.

## The analysis

The paper applies the sectoral systems of innovation framework to the
circular plastics economy, using its three building blocks:

**Knowledge and technical domains.** The knowledge base on digitalisation for
the African circular plastics economy is sparse. Where it exists, it points
to regulatory, economic and technical constraints as what blocks a circular
value chain from emerging. AI, mobile applications, IoT, 3D printing and GIS
are identified as the technologies most relevant to the transition.

**Actors and networks.** Organisations include technology-enabled waste
management firms, municipal collection companies, recyclers and sustainability
consultancies. Individuals include informal waste pickers, customers,
business owners and manufacturers. The dataset here is the organisational
half of that mapping.

**Institutions.** The norms, behaviours, laws and standards that shape how
the other actors interact.

The paper's argument is that the plastics problem is not solved by deploying
waste infrastructure and digital technology alone. A functioning circular
plastics value network in Sub-Saharan Africa requires integrated,
multi-sectoral and multi-stakeholder coordination, and the technology only
delivers inside that.
